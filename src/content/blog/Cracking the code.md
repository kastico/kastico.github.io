## What is cracking the code?

_The code for what?_ A better life? Becoming a better person and professional? Achieving my dreams while enjoying the journey? **For me, it's all of that.** I have a rough idea of where I want to go, but I don't have all the answers, and honestly, that's part of the fun.

I have the drive to do great things, but sometimes I forget what I'm capable of. **Getting started is often the hardest part**, but once I do, I give everything I've got. Sometimes that comes at a cost: I get so focused on my work or hobbies that I forget to give people the attention they deserve. It's never intentional, but it's something I need to work on.

My brain rarely switches off. I keep thinking about what I've learned, what I could improve, and what I want to learn next. _I always want more._

I'm not writing this for any particular reason. I just find it easier to understand my thoughts when I put them into words, and writing helps me slow down, reflect, and figure out what I really want. That's why I'm creating this space: a place to share what I learn, explore the things I'm curious about, and be honest about my feelings and struggles. I also want to track my progress and build a habit of showing up consistently.

---

### Part One — Drive & Consistency

My goal is to publish at least **twice a week**:

- **The weekly reflection** — what went well, what I struggled with, and what I've learned about myself.
- **The learning log** — something new I discovered that week, with examples, useful links, and code when relevant.

No big promises. Just learning, sharing, and getting better along the way.

> **Let's crack the code!**

# The Foundation

_Where do I start?_ I'm **26 years old**, and I've spent around 20 years in front of a computer: gaming, coding, producing, designing... you name it.

It's a bit of a cliché, but I was a shy kid. I had my older brother and my cousins, but outside that circle I didn't have many people around. My brother and I were both nerds, just different kinds.

I still remember when my parents bought a computer for the house. I didn't really know what I was looking at, but I was fascinated. I spent hours exploring, clicking around, and figuring things out, and a few months later, whenever something went wrong with the computer, **I was the person everyone called.** I also had a PS2, which I still own today, and that's where my love for gaming really grew. _Those were the days._

## From Playing Games to Creating Things

I've always been interested in more than just using technology. I love taking things apart, changing them, and making them my own.

It all really started when I illegally downloaded **GTA Vice City**. I discovered forums and websites where people shared custom mods for the game, and I was hooked. The idea that I could add my own touch to a game that so many people played was amazing, and knowing there was a whole community doing the same thing made it even better. I was around eight or nine years old, probably too young to be playing GTA, but hey, _I turned out alright... I think._

Back then, design came more naturally to me. I'd edit images, create skins, and experiment with different tools, and sometimes I'd even change bits of code, although usually with software that made it easier. **That curiosity never really left me.**

## Customization: A Love-Hate Relationship

Even today, I'm still a bit obsessed with customization. I love tweaking my setups and making everything feel like my own. As someone who loves design, I know this can be a bit counterproductive: sometimes, **less is more**, and adding too much can ruin a perfectly good setup. I get that. But I guess that's part of who I am. I like my workspace to feel like home, even if all those tweaks won't make me a better programmer. And honestly, there's something satisfying about having a setup that feels _uniquely yours_.

## The Terminal. End of Discussion.

I'm a terminal guy through and through, and I love it. I know there's a GUI for almost everything, but sometimes it's just unnecessary, and the terminal gets the job done without all the extra stuff. Funny enough, as someone who loves design, I can appreciate a good interface while still believing that **sometimes, the simplest solution is the best one.**

You won't change my mind about the terminal. _Forget it._
