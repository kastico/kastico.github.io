## Concept

**Balatros** is a typeface inspired by an English illuminated manuscript featuring the letter “T”. The original artwork had no recorded date or known author, but its distinctive forms provided the starting point for a new typeface while preserving and bringing renewed attention to the original design.

<Split image={inspiration} alt="Early type proofs in black and white" caption="Early black-and-white tests">
The organic shapes, elegant structure, and distinctive serifs of the illuminated “T” evoked the visual characteristics of court jesters, particularly the exaggerated forms of their traditional hats and shoes. This connection led to the name **Balastros**, a historical term associated with professional court jesters.
</Split>

The development of the typeface began by deconstructing the original illumination into a set of modular shapes. These elements became the foundation for constructing the rest of the alphabet, allowing the visual language of the original letterform to be extended into a complete type system.

## Shapes

Breaking the illumination down left me with a small kit of modular pieces. Everything else in the alphabet is built from them, like a set of type-shaped Lego.

<Figure src={i1} alt="Modular shapes derived from the illuminated T" caption="The kit of parts" maxWidth="800px" />

Of course, the original "T" never planned to become a full alphabet. Some letters and symbols couldn't be assembled from the modules alone, so I had to improvise. I tried to keep every intervention loyal to the source, somewhere between a historical reference and a contemporary typeface.
