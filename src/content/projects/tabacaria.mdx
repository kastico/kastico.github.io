## Concept

**Tabacaria** is an *editorial book* that interprets a poem by **Fernando Pessoa** through **typography, handwriting and abstract graphic marks**. It was created for a university project whose goal was to give students a **first hands-on introduction to the editorial field**: each student chose a poem and was free to portray it as a book in *any way they wanted*.


<Split image={capa} alt="Cover of the Tabacaria book" caption="The book" border={false}>
I chose "Tabacaria". Instead of illustrating it literally, I tried to show graphically what each word or phrase *feels* like, turning the poem's **doubt, emptiness and restlessness** into visual form.

The whole poem was **written by hand and then scanned**. Keeping the handwriting gave the text a fragile, personal voice, closer to *someone thinking out loud* than to a printed classic.
</Split>

## The Poem

"Tabacaria" was written by **Fernando Pessoa** under the heteronym **Álvaro de Campos** and first published in **1933**. It is one of the best-known poems in Portuguese. The speaker looks out of his window at the *tobacconist's shop across the street* and uses it as a starting point to reflect on who he is: someone who feels he is **nothing** and will never be anything, yet carries **all the dreams in the world**.

The poem moves between *grand metaphysical doubt* and *small everyday details*, between the inner world of dreams and the ordinary street outside.

 **The vast against the trivial. Being against not being.**
 That tension is what I tried to translate into the graphics.

## Process

### Handwriting

I wrote out the **complete poem by hand** and digitised **every page**. The irregularities of the pen (*hesitations, pressure changes, small errors*) were kept on purpose, since they carry part of the poem's mood.

### Graphics

The graphic elements come from two sources:

- **My own drawings**
- **Leftover screen-printing ink** that I had saved

These ink remnants, with their *stains, layers and accidental textures*, were scanned and reworked digitally into shapes that stand for the emotions in the poem. **Nothing is decorative:** each mark is tied to a word, a line or a shift in tone.

## The Book

Each spread pairs a passage of **handwritten text** with a **graphic that translates its feeling**, so the reading rhythm follows the *emotional rhythm of the poem* rather than a conventional layout.
